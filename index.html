About the workshop

A dedicated venue for diffusion language models

Autoregressive generation has dominated language modeling for years, but a fundamentally different paradigm is gaining rapid traction: diffusion language models.

Rather than generating tokens left-to-right, DLMs corrupt sequences through forward noising over categorical spaces and learn to reverse this corruption — enabling parallel decoding, bidirectional context, and fine-grained controllable generation.

In 2025–2026 this paradigm moved from theoretical curiosity to commercial reality: Inception Labs launched Mercury, the first commercial-scale DLM; academic labs released LLaDA and Dream; and work on SEDD, MDLM, FS-DFM, and LaViDa has shown DLMs can match or exceed autoregressive baselines while achieving up to 10× inference speedups.

This full-day workshop brings together researchers from academia and industry to consolidate theoretical understanding, benchmark competing approaches, and chart a roadmap for DLMs — catalyzing collaborations across the generative modeling, NLP, and systems communities.

01

Theory & Foundations

What is the right unifying formalism for diffusion language modeling? What compute-optimal scaling laws govern DLMs, and which architectural inductive biases matter most — trained from scratch or adapted from pretrained autoregressive models?

02

Efficiency

Which architectural designs best exploit DLM parallelism, and what is its practical upper bound at scale? How can DLMs support efficient long-context generation, and what systems and hardware support does real-world serving require?

03

Diffusion for Reasoning

Can the diversity advantage of diffusion be quantified and leveraged for test-time scaling? Is robustness to post-training collapse structural? Where is holistic refinement advantageous versus explicit sequential decomposition?

Topics of Interest

Beyond the three focal challenges above, we welcome contributions across a broad range of related topics, including but not limited to:

Categorical & masked diffusion formulations
Training objectives & probabilistic inference
Scaling laws & training efficiency

Submissions

Call for Papers

We invite submissions advancing diffusion language models and related non-autoregressive generative methods

Topics span theory, algorithms, and systems — including categorical and masked diffusion; training objectives and connections to probabilistic inference; sampling, parallel decoding, and efficient inference; reasoning, code generation, and planning; controllable generation and post-training; RL-based alignment; and hybrid autoregressive–diffusion approaches.

All submissions are non-archival and reviewed double-blind on OpenReview. Work published at NeurIPS 2026 or other archival venues is not eligible.

Extended Abstracts

Up to 4 pages + unlimited references. Work-in-progress, preliminary results, position papers.

Short Papers

Up to 8 pages + unlimited references. More complete contributions with experiments or theoretical results.

Each submission receives at least three reviews. We will award a Best Paper and a Best Student Paper prize. Organizers recuse themselves from submissions involving their own institution or active collaborators.
